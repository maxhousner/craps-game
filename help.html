How to play craps

Everything on the table, explained in plain English: what each spot is, when you can bet it, how it wins and loses, what it pays, and what it costs you on average (the house edge).

Craps in 60 seconds

  1. The come-out roll. Each round starts with a come-out roll, and the puck shows OFF.
  2. Naturals and craps. On the come-out, a 7 or 11 ("natural") wins for the Pass Line. A 2, 3 or 12 ("craps") loses. The round starts over either way.
  3. The point. Any other number (4, 5, 6, 8, 9 or 10) becomes the point. The puck flips to ON and sits on that number.
  4. Make it or seven out. The shooter keeps rolling. If the point rolls again, the Pass Line wins and a new come-out begins. If a 7 comes first, that's a seven out: the Pass Line loses and the dice go to the next shooter.
  5. Everything else on the table is a bet on what the dice will do along the way.

Why the numbers matter

Two dice can land 36 ways. The 7 can be made 6 ways, more than any other total, which is why so many bets are "this number before a 7."

Working, off and contract bets

A bet that's working wins or loses on the next roll. A bet that's off sits on the table untouched. These are the casino defaults on the come-out roll and once a point is set:

You can turn place, buy, lay, hardway and Big 6/8 bets (and come-bet odds) on or off at any time. A gray chip with an OFF tag isn't working; an ON tag means you turned it on for the come-out.

Contract bets

Once a point is set, Pass Line and Come bets can't be taken down. Their advantage comes from the come-out roll, where 7 and 11 win 8 ways and craps loses only 4. After that the bet is the underdog, so the casino holds you to it. The don't side can always come down, because doing so only helps the house. The Fire Bet and All/Tall/Small are also locked once rolling starts. Odds can always come down.

Proper units

Casinos pay in whole dollars. When a payout would include cents, the dealer rounds it down, which is called breakage. A $5 Place 6 should pay $5.83 at 7 to 6, but the casino pays $5. Betting in proper units avoids that.

By default this game only lets you roll with proper bets. In Settings you can allow other amounts; the payout then rounds down like a real table, and you'll still see a hint.

Field, Big 6/8, hardways, props and the side bets always pay whole dollars. Horn, World and C&E have to be bet in multiples of 4, 5 and 2 because they're split evenly across numbers.

House edge comparison

The house edge is the average share of each bet the casino keeps over time. A 1.41% edge means you lose about $1.41 per $100 bet in the long run. Lower is better. Short-term results swing far more than this, which is what makes strategy testing interesting.

Vegas defaults: vig charged on wins, field 12 pays 3 to 1, props pay "to 1". These figures are checked exactly by this game's test suite.

House rules (Settings)

Casinos vary, so you can set these to match the one you play. House rules can only change when no bets are on the table.

Table minimum / maximum
The smallest and largest bet on line, come, place, buy, lay, field and Big 6/8. Odds, hardways and props can go down to $1. The Place 6/8 minimum rounds up to a proper unit ($6 at a $5 table).
Max odds
How much odds you can take behind a line bet. "3-4-5x" is the Vegas standard. Higher odds lower your overall edge.

Charged only when the bet wins (cheaper) or up front when you make the bet.
Props pay
"To 1" (30 to 1, 15 to 1) or "for 1" (30 for 1 = 29 to 1). Affects the 2, 3, 11, 12, horn bets, C&E and hops.
All / Tall / Small
34/34/175 to 1 or the stingier 30/30/150.
Allow non-proper bets
Lets you roll with bets that aren't in proper units. Payouts round down.
Fast roll
Skips the dice animation, which is handy when you're testing a strategy.

Glossary

Bar
The number that's a push for don't bets on their first roll (usually 12).
Breakage
Rounding a payout down to the dollar when a bet isn't in proper units.
Center action / props
The one-roll and hardway bets in the middle of the table.
Collect / same bet
Take your winnings and leave the bet as it is.
Come-out roll
The first roll of a round, when no point is set (puck OFF).
Contract bet
A bet you can't take down: Pass Line and Come bets once they have a point.
Craps
A 2, 3 or 12. On the come-out it loses for the Pass Line.
Dark side / wrong bettor
Betting the don't side, against the shooter. "Right" bettors play pass and come.
Easy / hard
A 4, 6, 8 or 10 rolled without a pair (easy) or as a pair (hard).
Hedge
A bet that protects another, like Any Craps to cover a come-out Pass Line bet.
Hop
A one-roll bet on an exact dice combination.
Inside / across
Place bets on 5, 6, 8, 9 (inside) or on all six numbers (across).
Natural
A 7 or 11 on the come-out roll.
Odds
The zero-edge bet you add behind a line bet once it has a point.
Off / working
Whether a bet is in action on the next roll.
Parlay
Letting a bet ride with its winnings added.
Point
The 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 or 10 set on the come-out, which the shooter needs to roll again before a 7.
Press
Adding winnings to a bet to make it bigger, usually doubling it: "press my 6."
Proper units
Bet sizes that pay out in whole dollars, like $6 on the 6 or 8.
Puck
The ON/OFF marker showing whether a point is set and which number it is.
Regress
Lowering a bet after it wins to lock in profit, like dropping a $12 Place 6 to $6.
Seven out
A 7 after the point is set. The round ends and the dice pass to the next shooter.
Shooter
The player rolling the dice.
Stickman
The dealer who calls each roll and handles the center bets.
True odds
A payout that exactly matches the chance of winning, so there's no house edge.
Vig (vigorish)
The 5% commission on buy and lay bets.
Yo
The 11, called "yo-leven" so it isn't confused with "seven".
