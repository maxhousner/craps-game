How to play craps

Everything on the table, explained in plain English: what each spot is, when you can bet it, how it wins and loses, what it pays, and what it costs you on average (the house edge).

Craps in 60 seconds

  1. The come-out roll. Each round starts with a come-out roll, and the puck shows OFF.
  2. Naturals and craps. On the come-out, a 7 or 11 ("natural") wins for the Pass Line. A 2, 3 or 12 ("craps") loses. The round starts over either way.
  3. The point. Any other number (4, 5, 6, 8, 9 or 10) becomes the point. The puck flips to ON and sits on that number.
  4. Make it or seven out. The shooter keeps rolling. If the point rolls again, the Pass Line wins and a new come-out begins. If a 7 comes first, that's a seven out: the Pass Line loses and the dice go to the next shooter.
  5. Everything else on the table is a bet on what the dice will do along the way.

Why the numbers matter

Two dice can land 36 ways. The 7 can be made 6 ways, more than any other total, which is why so many bets are "this number before a 7."

Using this game

Placing bets

  • Bet: pick a chip in the rack ($1 to $1,000), then tap a spot. Tap again to add another chip. Stack different chips to make any amount.
  • More options: long-press a spot (phone) or right-click it (computer) to buy, lay or put a number, turn bets on or off, take them down, or open What's this? for help on that spot.
  • Red outline: that bet has to be fixed before you can roll (below the minimum, or not in proper units). Tap Fix. An amber outline is only a hint.
  • Undo takes back anything since the last roll. Take down removes every bet that's allowed to come down.

Around the table

  • The bar above the table shows the last roll and the stickman's call, your recent rolls, and your bankroll, money on the table, session result and the point.
  • My Bets lists every bet with what it wins if it hits, plus + − On/Off ✕ buttons. On a computer it's in the right-hand column under your session stats. On a phone, tap My Bets under the table.
  • Center action (the one-roll and hardway bets) sits in a felt strip under the table. Hide folds it away, and the bar still shows how much you have bet there. On a phone, tap Center Props under the table.
  • Session stats track your profit, rolls, shooters, points made and how often each number has rolled compared with the odds.

Tools

  • Settings (the gear, top right) holds the house rules, fast roll, and a bankroll reset. Your bankroll, bets and stats are saved in this browser.
  • Autoplay (the button next to your bankroll) bets and rolls a strategy for you on the table, at the speed you pick. Pause or stop any time; Space pauses.
  • Simulator runs strategies through thousands of sessions. See Using the simulator.
  • Keyboard: Space rolls, 1–6 pick a chip, ⌘/Ctrl+Z undoes.

Practice mode

Open the game with ?dev at the end of the address (craps-game/?dev) and a Force next roll picker appears under the chip rack. Pick both dice and every roll uses them until you set them back to "?". Center action & side bets

Working, off and contract bets

A bet that's working wins or loses on the next roll. A bet that's off sits on the table untouched. These are the casino defaults on the come-out roll and once a point is set:

You can turn place, buy, lay, hardway and Big 6/8 bets (and come-bet odds) on or off at any time. A gray chip with an OFF tag isn't working; an ON tag means you turned it on for the come-out.

Contract bets

Once a point is set, Pass Line and Come bets can't be taken down. Their advantage comes from the come-out roll, where 7 and 11 win 8 ways and craps loses only 4. After that the bet is the underdog, so the casino holds you to it. The don't side can always come down, because doing so only helps the house. The Fire Bet and All/Tall/Small are also locked once rolling starts. Odds can always come down.

Proper units

Casinos pay in whole dollars. When a payout would include cents, the dealer rounds it down, which is called breakage. A $5 Place 6 should pay $5.83 at 7 to 6, but the casino pays $5. Betting in proper units avoids that.

By default this game only lets you roll with proper bets. In Settings you can allow other amounts; the payout then rounds down like a real table, and you'll still see a hint.

House edge comparison

The house edge is the average share of each bet the casino keeps over time. A 1.41% edge means you lose about $1.41 per $100 bet in the long run. Lower is better. Short-term results swing far more than this, which is what makes strategy testing interesting.

Vegas defaults: vig charged on wins, field 12 pays 3 to 1, props pay "to 1". These figures are checked exactly by this game's test suite.

Smart play

No strategy beats the house edge over time: every roll is independent, and the dice don't remember what came before. What you control is which bets you make and how much you put in action. These habits keep the cost of playing low.

  • Build around the line. Bet the Pass Line (1.41%) or Don't Pass (1.36%), then take odds as soon as a point is set. Odds have no house edge, so the more of your money is in odds, the lower your overall edge: about 0.37% on the Pass Line with 3-4-5x odds.
  • Use come bets for more numbers. They work like the Pass Line, each with its own odds, so they keep the edge low while giving you more action.
  • If you place numbers, place the 6 and 8. Place 6 or 8 costs 1.52% (bet in $6 units). Place 5 or 9 costs 4%, and Place 4 or 10 costs 6.67%. Buying the 4 or 10 instead costs 1.67% when the vig is charged only on wins.
  • Skip Big 6 and Big 8. They win on exactly the same rolls as Place 6 and 8 but pay even money, so they cost 9.09% instead of 1.52%.
  • Treat center action as entertainment. Hardways and one-roll bets cost 9% to 17% (Any Seven is the worst at 16.67%), and the Fire Bet 20.76%. A small one now and then is fine; making them every roll adds up fast.
  • Watch your total action, not only the edge. On average you lose the house edge times everything you bet. Spreading $50 across the table costs about five times as much as $10 on the Pass Line, even when the edges are similar.
  • Betting systems don't change the edge. Pressing after wins or raising after losses changes how your results swing, not the average. Test a system in the simulator before trusting it.
  • Size your bankroll for the swings. Even low-edge play has long losing streaks. Set a session bankroll and a loss limit, and check the simulator's Worst drop and Ran out of money figures to see whether your bankroll can ride out a normal bad night.

Using the simulator

The simulator plays strategies through thousands of sessions in a few seconds, so you see the whole range of results a strategy produces instead of one lucky or unlucky night.

Setting it up

  • Strategies: add up to 4 to compare, from the popular presets or your own. Build your own opens the strategy builder, where you pick the bets, when they go up, and any presses or progressions. Strategies you save also show up in Autoplay.
  • Session: a starting bankroll, a session length (rolls, shooters, or hours at about 100 rolls each), and optional amounts to stop at when you're up or down.
  • Sessions to simulate: 1,000 for a quick look, up to 50,000 for the most precise averages.
  • Dice seed: every strategy gets exactly the same dice, session by session, so any difference comes from the strategy, not luck. The same seed always gives the same results; New dice picks a fresh set.
  • House rules start from your table's settings, and you can change them for a run.

Reading the results

  • Average result is what a strategy wins or loses per session in the long run. Median is the typical session.
  • Middle 50% / 90% show the swings to expect, and Chance to finish ahead is how often a session ends up.
  • House edge you paid is total lost divided by total bet. Ran out of money is how often the bankroll couldn't cover the next bet.
  • A ★ marks the best strategy on each row.
  • The charts show where sessions ended, how the bankroll moved during a session, and how results are spread. Replay a session steps through any one session roll by roll, and the CSV buttons export everything.

House rules (Settings)

Casinos vary, so you can set these to match the one you play. House rules can only change when no bets are on the table.

Table minimum / maximum
The smallest and largest bet on line, come, place, buy, lay, field and Big 6/8. Odds, hardways and props can go down to $1. The Place 6/8 minimum rounds up to a proper unit ($6 at a $5 table).
Max odds
How much odds you can take behind a line bet. "3-4-5x" is the Vegas standard. Higher odds lower your overall edge.
Field: 12 pays
3 to 1 (2.78% edge) or 2 to 1 (5.56%).
Don't side pushes on
Bar 12 (most casinos) or bar 2. The edge is the same.
Buy / lay vig
Charged only when the bet wins (cheaper) or up front when you make the bet.
Props pay
"To 1" (30 to 1, 15 to 1) or "for 1" (30 for 1 = 29 to 1). Affects the 2, 3, 11, 12, horn bets, C&E and hops.
All / Tall / Small
34/34/175 to 1 or the stingier 30/30/150.
Allow non-proper bets
Lets you roll with bets that aren't in proper units. Payouts round down.
Fast roll
Skips the dice animation, which is handy when you're testing a strategy.

Glossary

Bar
The number that's a push for don't bets on their first roll (usually 12).
Breakage
Rounding a payout down to the dollar when a bet isn't in proper units.
Center action / props
The one-roll and hardway bets in the middle of the table.
Collect / same bet
Take your winnings and leave the bet as it is.
Come-out roll
The first roll of a round, when no point is set (puck OFF).
Contract bet
A bet you can't take down: Pass Line and Come bets once they have a point.
Craps
A 2, 3 or 12. On the come-out it loses for the Pass Line.
Dark side / wrong bettor
Betting the don't side, against the shooter. "Right" bettors play pass and come.
Easy / hard
A 4, 6, 8 or 10 rolled without a pair (easy) or as a pair (hard).
Hedge
A bet that protects another, like Any Craps to cover a come-out Pass Line bet.
Hop
A one-roll bet on an exact dice combination.
Inside / across
Place bets on 5, 6, 8, 9 (inside) or on all six numbers (across).
Natural
A 7 or 11 on the come-out roll.
Odds
The zero-edge bet you add behind a line bet once it has a point.
Off / working
Whether a bet is in action on the next roll.
Parlay
Letting a bet ride with its winnings added.
Point
The 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 or 10 set on the come-out, which the shooter needs to roll again before a 7.
Press
Adding winnings to a bet to make it bigger, usually doubling it: "press my 6."
Proper units
Bet sizes that pay out in whole dollars, like $6 on the 6 or 8.
Puck
The ON/OFF marker showing whether a point is set and which number it is.
Regress
Lowering a bet after it wins to lock in profit, like dropping a $12 Place 6 to $6.
Seven out
A 7 after the point is set. The round ends and the dice pass to the next shooter.
Shooter
The player rolling the dice.
Stickman
The dealer who calls each roll and handles the center bets.
True odds
A payout that exactly matches the chance of winning, so there's no house edge.
Vig (vigorish)
The 5% commission on buy and lay bets.
Yo
The 11, called "yo-leven" so it isn't confused with "seven".
